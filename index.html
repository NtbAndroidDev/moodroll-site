Moodroll

A mood journal made of photographs.

Nothing you write or shoot leaves your phone.

A month, one colour a day. The empty squares are the point — a gallery cannot draw the days you did not write.

A diary worth reopening

Most mood trackers record a day as an emoji and a line of text, and there is nothing to come back to. This one records it as a photograph.

Give the day one of seven colours, add a note if you want to, and weeks later the timeline is a grid of colour — the shape of a month, readable without a word of it. A day can be recorded with no photograph at all, and many are.

It never nags

  • The streak counts days and never counts down. Miss one and it is forgiven, twice a month. Today never counts against you, because the day is not over.
  • One reminder, once a day, and it skips a day you have already recorded. A colour on its own is enough — no photograph required. Turn it off and nothing chases you.
  • Insight counts your days back to you. Counts, only. No score, no trend warning, and no advice about your own life.

24 cameras, not 24 filters

This is where the photographs come from, and it is why the diary is worth reopening. Each look is rendered per shot — grain, halation, lens softness, vignette — rather than stamped over a finished image.

Several of them behave like the real thing, too. The single-use camera gives you 27 frames before you load a new roll. The instant cameras print inside a white border. The early digicam shoots at its own small sensor size, so the softness is in the picture instead of on it.

The shutter has three settings and no menu behind them. Auto takes one frame and sets the lens itself. Portrait hands you the aperture ring. Double exposure lays two frames on one negative, with seven ways for the two to meet.

The border is yours as well. Keep the one the camera prints, or put every photograph inside a square instant, a mini instant or a 35 mm film strip — the viewfinder reframes to match, so what you compose is what the print keeps.

A camera can be sent to somebody else as a short code or a small file — including one you built yourself. Opening one costs nothing and needs no subscription, on any tier.

The camera is a Quick Settings tile too, so the viewfinder is one tap from the notification shade.

Your diary stays on your phone

Your photos, moods and notes never leave this phone. There is no account, no server, and no cloud sync — and the diary is excluded from Android's automatic Google Drive backup, which is on by default for most apps.

Moodroll does not measure how you use it. There is no usage analytics in the app at all — not which screens you open, not which cameras you use, not whether you bought anything. Crash reports do go to Google Firebase, so that bugs can be found and fixed on phones we cannot hold; they never contain a photograph, a note, or which mood you tagged. No advertising identifier is collected.

Location tagging is off by default. Switch it on and the coordinates are written into the photograph's own file, on your phone — they are never uploaded, and a PNG export carries none.

All of it is written out in full, in plain language, in the privacy policy.

No ads. Not now, not later.
