How it works

Ten seconds a day.

Most days are a colour and nothing else, and the diary is built for that.

  1. Pick a camera

    Swipe the shelf and choose by eye — every camera shows a frame it made before you load it.

  2. Keep the day

    One of seven colours and how strongly. A photo, a note or a tag only if you feel like it.

  3. Read it back

    Weeks later the timeline is a grid of colour — the shape of a month, readable without a word.

Two taps

The day is kept before you lock the phone

One of seven colours, and how strongly. A note if you want one, a tag if you want one, and a photograph only if you feel like taking one.

One day

Go back and say more

A day holds its photographs, what it felt like, and anything you want to write down. Nothing is locked once it is saved — add the sentence you could not find at the time, or develop the same frame again on another camera.

Patterns

It counts. It never judges.

Insight counts your days back to you and stops there — no score, no trend warning, no advice about your own life. The streak counts days and never counts down: miss one and it is forgiven, twice a month.

Yours alone

Nothing leaves your phone

No account, no server, no cloud sync — and the diary is excluded from Android's automatic Google Drive backup. There is no usage analytics in the app at all. Crash reports go to Google Firebase so bugs can be fixed; they never carry a photograph, a note, or a mood.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is Moodroll free?

Yes. The free app keeps your whole diary forever and includes three full-quality cameras, Insight, the month widget, gallery import, search and backups. Pro unlocks the rest of the 30 cameras and a handful of extras.

Where are my photos and notes stored?

On your phone, and only there. There is no account and no server holding your diary. You can save a copy to a file whenever you like and restore it on another phone.

Does it need an internet connection?

No. The camera and the diary work with no connection at all. A connection is only used for buying Pro through Google Play and for crash reports, which never contain a photo, a note or a mood.

Are these real film cameras?

They are looks built to behave like the real thing: grain, halation, lens softness and vignette rendered per shot. Some even run out — the single-use camera gives you 27 frames before you load a new roll.

What do I need to run it?

Android 7.0 or newer, and a camera.

Will there be ads?

No. Not now, not later.
